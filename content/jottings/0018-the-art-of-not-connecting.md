I have booked a holiday to Germany, seduced once again by the quiet charm of trundling through narrow, winding valleys on little local trains.

Long-distance rail across the border, however, has evolved into an exercise in temporal suspension. One does not simply travel; one sets aside a calendar day and surrenders to fate. Delays are so reliably catastrophic that allowing a generous sixty minutes for a connection—not across town, but between adjacent platforms in the very same station—feels like a reckless gamble. The schedule provides only a theoretical opening bid; the actual arrival remains a private matter between Deutsche Bahn and the universe.

To keep my blood pressure within civilised limits, I chose a route anchored by one solitary ICE. Sheer logistical modesty. No high‑stakes dash down echoing stairwells, no existential limbo on an unfamiliar platform. Just one long glide across the country, followed by a modest local train that feels more like a small reward for not overreaching.

Even so, part of me looks forward to the inevitable pause on a draughty provincial platform, waiting for the tannoy to crackle into life: “The 10:19 service to Lüdenscheid will depart today at 10:51.” Announced with such calm assurance, it always feels less like an apology and more like an unexpected little gift, wrapped especially for me.
