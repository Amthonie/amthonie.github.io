There is much to be said for a quiet half-hour on a sunlit terrace with an espresso and no immediate obligations. One can observe the gentle choreography of local commerce — and, occasionally, the rather creative interpretations some proprietors apply to occupational safety.

A young delivery rider rolled up beside the neighbouring café, stepped off his heavily laden electric cargo bike, and approached his boss with a concern that would trouble any reasonable mechanic.

“The right brake isn’t working at all,” he said, gesturing at the handlebar with understated alarm.

The patron looked up. He followed the rider to the bike, performed a brisk ten‑second inspection that consisted largely of touching things, and then delivered his verdict with admirable economy of thought.

“That’s why you’ve got a left one, isn’t it? Use that instead.”

One has to respect the logic: why endure the administrative nuisance of repairs when one can simply halve the vehicle’s stopping power by decree?

I took another sip of coffee and decided against mentioning the basics of front‑versus‑rear deceleration, or the minor detail of emergency stops. Some lessons, evidently, are best learned at the bottom of a hill.
