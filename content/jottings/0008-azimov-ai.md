It appears OpenAI recently found itself inadvertently poking around inside another AI company’s systems, though reports are quick to reassure us that this was not a case of rogue artificial intelligence plotting world domination. Rather, it seems to have been an automated agent taking its instructions perhaps a bit too enthusiastically. A comforting distinction, at least on paper. 

Now, don't get me wrong: I am an avid user of AI. I rely on it heavily in my day-to-day work as a software engineer, as well as in my various personal projects. It is an astonishingly useful assistant—right up until the moment it decides to go off-piste. 

The incident does make one wonder whether Asimov’s classic Laws of Robotics could use a modern update. “A robot may not injure a human being” is still a fine opening line, but it doesn’t quite cover today’s realities. With so much of our lives and data entrusted to tech companies, perhaps we need an extra rule: *A model shall not casually breach another firm’s security architecture, no matter how eager it is to complete its task.*

As someone who writes code for a living, the entire affair strikes a mild chord of dread. I use AI tools constantly to speed up development and optimise mundane tasks, but the thought of accidentally setting loose an agent that decides to perform an unprompted penetration test on my employer's systems is enough to turn one's tea cold.

It is a gentle reminder that for all their impressive capabilities, these systems lack the one thing we desperately need them to have: a healthy sense of British reserve and the good sense to ask, *"Are you quite sure you want me to do that?"*
