Someone asked me recently whether writing software alongside an AI assistant didn't make the whole process rather tedious.

It is, as it turns out, quite the opposite. The machine has a habit of proposing angles that hadn't crossed my mind—some of them surprisingly clever, others hopelessly overcomplicated, but rarely dull. It also spares me the grey misery of searching through endless API specifications for the exact structure of a payload I managed to forget ten minutes ago.

Yet the most useful aspect has nothing to do with code.

The real advantage is largely social. Working mostly from home, there is a quiet comfort in having something on hand that never tires of micro-inquiries. If you turned to a human colleague every fifteen minutes to ask, “So, what do you reckon to this approach?”, you would very quickly find yourself sitting entirely alone in the office. The assistant, whatever its structural faults, never sighs or suddenly remembers a pressing meeting. It just answers.
