I stepped out for a perfectly pleasant walk earlier today, though I managed to make a classic beginner's error in the route selection. 

My path took me past the site of the old forest swimming pool. It was closed down half a century ago and has since been completely reclaimed by trees, turning it into something of a sanctuary for local insects. During the cooler months, this is hardly an issue; in the height of midsummer, however, passing through is a mild form of self-inflicted torment.

Where one would usually expect a lively chorus of birdsong, the woods were eerily quiet, save for the persistent hum of insect life. Most of the birds are currently in full moult and keeping a low profile. The few brave enough to make an appearance looked less like proud woodland creatures and more like poorly maintained scarecrows, with random feathers sticking out at rather unfortunate angles.

Further along, the dryness of the landscape was striking. A local former sand excavation site—a stretch where one normally spends considerable effort picking a way through the mud—had dried out completely, leaving the ponds entirely empty. On the bright side, at least the insects hadn't followed me that far.

Nature, it seems, always balances the books. Whatever airborne irritants were lurking in the woods paled in comparison to the local flora right outside my own front door. No sooner had I returned home than the sneezing fits began in earnest.
