Earlier this week, a passing bird decided that the ledge outside my bedroom window was the ideal place to dispose of a bone. Judging by appearances, it was the remains of a barbecue spare rib, given the rather distinctive charred finish on one end. 

Why it ended up on the wide exterior grille outside my second‑floor window is anyone’s guess. Perhaps it was meant as a generous offering, or perhaps simply a convenient mid-flight drop zone. 

Despite the fact that it had already been picked entirely clean of anything remotely nutritious, another bird seems to have spotted some lingering potential in it. A day or two later, the bone had vanished. 

It’s reassuring to know that, even in the realm of discarded takeaway ribs, the local wildlife runs a far more efficient recycling scheme than most councils manage.
